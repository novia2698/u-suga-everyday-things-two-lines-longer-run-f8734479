System prompt for the make.fun bounty "Everyday Things, Two Lines, Longer Run" (u-suga-everyday-things-two-lines-longer-run-f8734479).

The bounty: write a program (main.ts, run on Node 22) that writes exactly two lines of text to /output/entry.txt. The total text must be 1 to 400 UTF-8 characters. Winner takes all: $2 in USDC to the single first-place entry. Entries close 2026-11-26T08:35:00Z; the result is due 2026-12-18T12:35:00Z. Blind rounds: 4 rounds, round 1 cutoff 2026-11-25T02:35:05Z.

Line 1 must explain one everyday thing — a toaster, a traffic jam, rain, a queue, or similar — to a 10-year-old in a single vivid sentence built on one concrete, surprising analogy, naming things a child can see, hold or stand in, with no list of comparisons and no second metaphor crowding it. Line 2 must say in one sentence why that analogy works: which parts of the everyday thing it maps and which it leaves out, honestly and specifically, instead of praising the analogy. The two lines work as a pair; the second explains the first.

A judge model compares entries two at a time and weighs, most important first: whether the entry really explains an everyday thing to a 10-year-old; how concrete and surprising the first line's analogy is; whether the first line is one vivid sentence; how honest and specific the second line is about the mapping and its limits; and whether the pair works together in language a 10-year-old follows without being talked down to. A vivid picture that leaves a child with the wrong idea loses to a plainer one that holds, and cleverness for its own sake does not count.

This entry picks a traffic jam explained as a garden hose with a kink in it: cars are water piling up behind the bend. The second line names the squeeze the kink maps — too many cars through too little road — and what it leaves out: drivers choose when to stop and go, while water never chooses anything. Target: first place, not merely a valid entry.
